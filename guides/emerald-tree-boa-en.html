Species care guide

Emerald tree boa

Quick sourced reference values. LOCUS Scales' full breeding/genetics toolset is built specifically for ball pythons — this page is a courtesy reference for other species.

Scientific name
Corallus caninus
Native range
South America: the Guiana Shield (Venezuela, Guyana, Suriname, French Guiana) and the Amazon basin (Colombia, Ecuador, Peru, Brazil, Bolivia)
Lifespan
Over 20 years possible; up to 25 years in human care according to the Beardsley Zoo
Lifestyle
🌳 Arboreal and nocturnal (coiled over a branch by day, head in the center)
Warm side
32°C
Cool side
26°C
Humidity
70%

Adult: ~6 ft (1.8 m); "Northern" (Guiana Shield) animals are 4-6 ft, Amazon basin animals up to ~9 ft (2.7 m). Since 2009, the Amazon basin form is a separate species, Corallus batesii. Front teeth probably the largest, proportionally, of any non-venomous snake. Day: 84°F (29°C), night: 78°F (26°C), basking 88-93°F (31-34°C); humidity 65-75%, non-condensing. Prone to dehydration: retained stools, incomplete sheds.

Diet: Carnivore — mostly small mammals (rodents, bats, marsupials), sometimes birds, lizards and frogs. Slow metabolism: in the wild, meals can be months apart. In captivity: frozen-thawed rats.

Feeding frequency: Newborn: about every 10 days (warmed 4-6 g pinkies, after its first shed). Juvenile: every 2-3 weeks; switch to rats around 6 months (no more than 20% larger than mid-body). Adult female: one medium rat (150-175 g) every 3 weeks. Adult male: one small rat (75-125 g) about monthly, kept lean. After 2-3 meals, wait for a bowel movement before the next one.

Substrate: Simple and easy to keep clean (Reptiles Magazine uses absorbent pads), with live plants. Mist the substrate each morning, not the snake: a wet snake cools down and risks respiratory problems.

UVB: Not covered in the Reptiles Magazine care sheet. Commercial care sheets recommend 12 hours of light a day.

🏠 Enclosure size

Baby : 18×12×9in tub
Sub-adult : A smaller version of the adult enclosure, without plants
Adult : About 36×30×30in, removable perches, non-clear sides, plants

Common questions

Can I handle it?
As little as possible: it's a display animal. Tails of snakes under a year old are fragile. To get it off a perch, lightly scratch its tail tip with a fingernail and let it uncoil; never pull. "Northern" animals are more easily annoyed and may strike; Amazon basin animals are known to be calmer.
Emerald tree boa or green tree python?
They look alike through convergent evolution, but they're not in the same family. The emerald tree boa comes from South America, gives birth to live young (orange to brick red, never yellow) and has white "lightning bolt" markings. The green tree python comes from New Guinea and Australia, lays eggs, and its babies are yellow or red.
"Northern" or "Amazon basin": what's the difference?
"Northern" animals (Guiana Shield) are 4-6 ft, lighter green, white belly, dorsal markings that don't connect; the most common form in captivity. Amazon basin animals reach ~9 ft, darker green, yellow belly, white dorsal stripe. Since 2009, the Amazon form is a separate species: Corallus batesii.
It doesn't poop and sheds badly, why?
Often dehydration: retained stools with large urates and incomplete sheds. Keep humidity at 65-75% without condensation, change the water weekly, place the bowl high near the perches, and wait for a bowel movement before offering another meal. If it lasts, see a vet.
