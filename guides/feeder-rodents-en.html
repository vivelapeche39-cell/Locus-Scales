📖 Guide: starting and running a colony

🐭 Mice, rats or ASF: which one?

The right rodent is first the one that's the right size for your snakes. Mice cover hatchlings and small snakes; rats take over as the snake grows. ASF (African soft-furred rats, Mastomys) sit in between. Check the prey size lexicon to match sizes to weights.

👨‍👩‍👧 Ratios: pair, trio or harem

Three classic ways to set up a breeding bin:

  • Pair (1 male : 1 female). The easiest to track: you always know which female had which litter.
  • Trio (1 male : 2 females). More pups per male, and in mice the mothers help raise each other's litters.
  • Harem (1 male : 3–4 females). The most output per male, but the bin fills up fast and tracking gets harder.

To start, lab references recommend at least 2 to 4 breeding pairs rather than one: a colony that depends on a single male stops the day he has a problem.

📅 The pace: why it overflows so fast

In both mice and rats, the female becomes fertile again within 24 hours of giving birth. If the male stays in, she can be pregnant again while nursing, so litters arrive about 3 weeks apart. Great for output, but you have to wean the previous litter before the next one is born, or the older pups trample the newborns and the bin overflows.

For rats, the MSD Veterinary Manual even recommends letting the female rest at least 2 months between litters, because being pregnant and nursing at the same time wears her down. If you leave the male in permanently, watch the females' condition and plan breaks.

🍼 Weaning and separating the sexes

  • Wean at about 21 days for mice and rats (up to 28 days for small mouse pups). Pups must eat solid food and reach water on their own.
  • Separate the sexes at weaning. A young male rat can sire a litter as early as 5 weeks, though it's uncommon; mice are adults at about 6 weeks. Waiting risks surprise litters between siblings.
  • Breeding age: mice from 6–8 weeks; rats usually from 3–4 months.
  • Replacing breeders: mouse output drops with age; lab references replace breeders at about 7–8 months. In rats, the usual breeding window runs from 3–4 months to 10–12 months.

🧬 Avoiding inbreeding

A closed colony always ends up related: a male's daughters stay with him, his sons start a new bin with their sisters… In mice, references note that inbred lines have smaller litters than outbred ones. For a feeder colony, that means fewer prey for the same work.

  • Record where every breeder comes from (which bin, which litter).
  • Never put a male back with his own daughters or sisters.
  • Bring in a breeder from another source now and then for fresh blood.

That's exactly where paper falls short: after a few months, nobody remembers who came from where.

❓ Frequently asked questions

How many mice do I need to feed a snake?

It depends on how many snakes you keep, their size and how often they eat. Start by counting how many prey you use per month, then adjust the number of breeding bins. With litters about every 3 weeks per female, a few trios are often enough for a small collection; LOCUS then shows what each bin really produces.

When should baby rats be separated?

At weaning, around 21 days. Separate males from females then: a young male can sire a litter as early as 5 weeks.

Can I leave the male with the females all the time?

Yes, it's common with mice and gives the most litters, but you have to wean each litter before the next is born. For rats, the MSD Veterinary Manual recommends at least 2 months of rest between litters for the female's health.

How do I avoid inbreeding in my rodent colony?

Record where every breeder comes from, never put a male back with his daughters or sisters, and regularly bring in a breeder from another source. LOCUS warns you before a related pairing.
