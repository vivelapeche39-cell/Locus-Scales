African house snake

Quick sourced reference values. LOCUS Scales' full breeding/genetics toolset is built specifically for ball pythons — this page is a courtesy reference for other species.

Scientific name
Boaedon fuliginosus (anciennement Lamprophis fuliginosus)
Native range
Sub-Saharan Africa: savanna, scrub, woodland, grassland, farmland and the outskirts of villages
Lifespan
15 to 20 years in captivity (Reptile Direct)
Lifestyle
🌍 Terrestrial and nocturnal
Warm side
32°C
Cool side
23°C
Humidity
45%

Adult male: 2-3 ft (60-90 cm). Female: 3-4½ ft (90-135 cm), heavier-bodied. Basking spot 88-92°F (31-33°C), warm side 80-85°F, cool side 70-75°F (never below 68°F), night 65-72°F; humidity 40-60% according to Dubia.com (30-50% according to Reptile Direct). Young ones escape easily.

Diet: Carnivore: small mammals, birds and lizards in the wild. In captivity: frozen-thawed mice, small rats for big females. Prey about as wide as the thickest part of the body.

Feeding frequency: Baby (under 4 months): pinky or fuzzy mouse every 5-7 days. Juvenile (4 months to 1 year): every 7 days. Adult male: adult mouse every 7-10 days. Adult female: adult mouse to small rat every 7-14 days.

Substrate: Soil or a mix that holds some moisture (at least 2 in), with a humid hide lined with moss. Remove waste daily.

UVB: Not strictly required, but recommended (low UVB, about 12 hours of light a day).

🏠 Enclosure size

Baby : 10-gallon tank or small tub, under 18 in long
Sub-adult : 20-gallon long, 18 to 30 in long
Adult : Male: 24×18×18 in. Female: 36×18×18 in, ideally 36×24×18 in

Common questions

Is it a good first snake?
Yes, it's considered a beginner species: hardy, small, a good eater. Young ones may strike, musk and thrash, but this settles with gentle, regular handling; adults are usually calm.
Can I keep two together?
No, because of the risk of cannibalism. One snake per enclosure.
My female keeps laying eggs, is that a problem?
Yes, it can wear her out: this species needs no cool period and can lay about every 60 days. Separate the male and give her at least 6 months of rest a year so she regains weight.
Why do some care sheets say Lamprophis?
That's the old genus name. The African house snake is now Boaedon fuliginosus; it's the same animal.
