Honduran milk snake

Quick sourced reference values. LOCUS Scales' full breeding/genetics toolset is built specifically for ball pythons — this page is a courtesy reference for other species.

Scientific name
Lampropeltis abnorma (vendu sous le nom L. triangulum hondurensis)
Native range
Central America: low to moderate elevations in Nicaragua, northeastern Costa Rica and the Caribbean slope of Honduras
Lifespan
12 to 20 years in captivity (Reptiles Magazine)
Lifestyle
🌍 Terrestrial and nocturnal
Warm side
30°C
Cool side
26°C
Humidity
50%

One of the largest milk snakes: about 48 in (1.2 m) on average, some reaching 5 ft (1.5 m) in captivity. Warm side mid- to upper-80s °F (29-31°C), cool side 78-82°F (26-28°C), night down to the low 70s °F; humidity 40-60%. Since Ruane et al. (2014) it is placed in the species Lampropeltis abnorma, but the trade still uses "hondurensis". Known phases: normal, "tangerine", and several mutations (albino, anerythristic, hypo, snow, ghost, striped).

Diet: Carnivore: rodents, birds, lizards, frogs and other snakes in the wild. In captivity: frozen-thawed mice; the largest adults take small to medium rats.

Feeding frequency: Hatchling: thawed pinky, 10 to 14 days after hatching (after the first shed); the Honduran readily takes rodents. Juveniles, males and off-season adults: one meal a week. Adult female in breeding season: twice a week.

Substrate: Aspen shavings (preferred) or coconut fiber. One hide on the warm side and one on the cool side, always fresh water.

UVB: Not needed (nocturnal species); an incandescent lamp can help keep the temperature.

🏠 Enclosure size

Baby : Small ventilated, escape-proof tub
Sub-adult : Medium tub or terrarium
Adult : At least the equivalent of a 29-gallon aquarium

Common questions

Does it handle well?
Babies are often nervous and squirmy, but most calm down with age and become good handling snakes.
Can I keep two together?
No: milk snakes and kingsnakes are cannibalistic. Keep them apart, except for supervised breeding.
Hondurensis or abnorma: which name is right?
Same animal. Since a 2014 genetic study (Ruane et al.), the milk snake was split into 7 species and the Honduran belongs to Lampropeltis abnorma. Breeders and stores still often use the old name L. t. hondurensis.
It sheds in pieces, what should I do?
Raise humidity a little during the shed: a bigger water bowl or a humid hide (damp moss). Keep overall humidity between 40 and 60%.
