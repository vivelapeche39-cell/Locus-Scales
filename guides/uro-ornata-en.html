Ornate Uromastyx (Sinai)

Quick sourced reference values. LOCUS Scales' full breeding/genetics toolset is built specifically for ball pythons — this page is a courtesy reference for other species.

Scientific name
Uromastyx ornata
Native range
Sinai Peninsula and Arabian coastal regions
Lifespan
15-20 years
Lifestyle
🌍 Terrestrial, burrowing

Diet: Strictly herbivorous — dark leafy greens, legumes/seeds, occasional edible flowers. No insects recommended, even for juveniles, unlike some other uromastyx.

Feeding frequency: Juvenile: greens daily. Adult: greens every 2 days.

Substrate: Sand or sand/soil mix, deep enough to allow digging.

UVB: Essential, strong UVB — the high basking target applies at full strength even though the animal is smaller than other uromastyx (digestion doesn't scale down with body size).

Incubation: Oviparous species. ~58-92 days depending on incubation temperature.

Clutches per mating: Generally 1 clutch per successful mating per season.
Average egg count: Typically under a dozen — smaller clutches than the genus's larger species.

Warm side
52°C
Cool side
29°C
Humidity
20%

The smallest and most colorful of the commonly kept uromastyx — males vivid green/blue/red with banding, females noticeably duller (marked sexual dichromatism). Often chosen for this reason rather than for a smaller enclosure — the enclosure still needs to be generous.

🏠 Enclosure size — Baby/juvenile: 30×18×18in · Sub-adult: 36×18×18in · Adult: 48×24×18in minimum
Source: reptilesencaptivite.com

Common questions

It's smaller — can I lower the basking temperature?
No, and this is a real source of confusion. Its smaller body size doesn't change the thermal needs for digesting a fibrous plant diet — keep the same extreme hot spot as the genus's larger species.
Why do the male and female look so different?
Marked sexual dichromatism — males show vivid colors (green, blue, red) with contrasting bands, females stay much duller in brown/pale yellow. Normal for the species, not a sign of illness in the female.
Do I really need to feed it insects?
No, even less so than other uromastyx — recent veterinary sources specifically advise against insects for this species, regardless of age.
