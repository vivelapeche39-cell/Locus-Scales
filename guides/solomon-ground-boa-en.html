Solomon Island ground boa

Quick sourced reference values. LOCUS Scales' full breeding/genetics toolset is built specifically for ball pythons — this page is a courtesy reference for other species.

Scientific name
Candoia paulsoni
Native range
Solomon Islands, Papua New Guinea and eastern Indonesia
Lifespan
Up to 35 years for the Candoia genus
Lifestyle
🌍 Terrestrial (likes to burrow, also uses branches)
Warm side
28°C
Cool side
25°C
Humidity
70%

Adult: about 1 m (3.5 ft). Day 27-28°C (80-82°F), night 24-27°C (75-80°F), humidity 60-80%. Several values on this sheet come from sources covering the whole Candoia genus (Pacific island boas). Newborns are very small and fragile. Wild-caught animals may bite; captive-born ones rarely do.

Diet: Carnivore — appropriately sized mice, frozen-thawed. Newborns are often picky: they prefer small frogs and lizards and often refuse pinkies at first. Scenting the pinky with a frog or lizard helps.

Feeding frequency: Baby and juvenile: every 7-10 days (one breeder waits ~3 weeks after birth before the first meal). Adult: every 10-14 days according to one source, every 3-4 weeks according to a specialist breeder — adjust to the animal's condition.

Substrate: Cypress mulch (holds humidity and allows burrowing). Newborns on paper towels. A large water bowl: these boas drink and soak often. Never pine or cedar — toxic oils.

UVB: Not documented as essential in the sources consulted.

🏠 Enclosure size

Baby : Small tub (shoebox)
Sub-adult : Upsize gradually: enclosure about as long as the snake, and half as deep
Adult : ~36×18in floor space for an adult of about 1 m, with hides and a large water bowl

Common questions

My newborn refuses to eat, is that normal?
Yes, it's the most common problem with this species. In the wild, the young mostly eat frogs and lizards. Wait a few weeks after birth, then offer a pinky scented with a frog or lizard.
Does it bite?
Wild-caught animals can be nervous and may bite. Captive-born ones rarely do.
It soaks in its water bowl a lot, is that bad?
No: Candoia boas drink and soak often. Provide a large bowl of clean water. If it soaks constantly, still check the temperature and look for mites.
